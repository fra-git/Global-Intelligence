### Electrolux deal at the industry ministry nearly halves job cuts
At the Mimit, the Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy, unions Fiom, Fim and Uilm signed an agreement with Electrolux. Redundancies fall from over 1,700 to just over 900. Investment rises to €100m and output is set at 3.2 million units. The Cerreto d'Esi plant stays open until it is reindustrialised. Exit incentives reach up to €95,000. The government has promised to refinance the wage-support schemes and "expansion contracts" (early-retirement tools).

**Why it matters:** Home appliances are a core Italian manufacturing sector, and this is one of the few recent restructurings where job cuts were cut back.

## Italy in the EU & PNRR

The Commission has already given a favourable opinion on Italy's request to use the EU's "national escape clause". This lets a country spend more on defence and energy without counting it against its deficit limits. Finance ministers are due to confirm it at the Ecofin meeting on 9 November. Reports differ on the exact date, so check the Council's calendar.

### Escape clause: Council vote on 9 November
Rome applied on 11 September, and the Commission gave a positive opinion on 8 October. Italy's parliament authorised the use of the clause on 13 October. The government indicates about 0.3% of GDP each for defence and energy in 2027 and in 2028, within an overall ceiling of 1.5% of GDP. Reports put the sums at roughly €14bn, but they do not agree. Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti had not yet decided how to use the room as of 2 November. One report puts the Commission's opinion on Italy's budget plan at 24 November, which I could not confirm.

**Watch:** The 9 November Ecofin, then the Commission's opinion on Italy's draft budget.

**For Europe & Italy:** Weak Chinese demand hurts German carmakers and their Italian suppliers. China is also pushing the EU toward a broader debate on cutting its dependence on China.

## Russia & Ukraine

The FT reports that US, UK, EU and Nato officials will meet in Brussels this week ahead of talks in Abu Dhabi. These would be the first US talks with Russia and Ukraine since February. Earlier reporting put the Abu Dhabi round at around 10-11 November. Moscow's attendance is not guaranteed.

**Why it matters:** The earlier rounds this year, in Abu Dhabi and Geneva, produced no deal. Europeans are trying to coordinate positions before Washington sits down with Moscow. Handelsblatt argues Germany is Kyiv's biggest backer but that Ukraine's fate is being decided in Moscow and Washington.

### Russian strikes kill dozens

The BBC reports that at least 38 people were killed in 24 hours after Russian attacks on an aid centre, a hospice and a hospital. Zelensky warned that dozens more could be trapped under rubble. This follows the strike on the aid point in Komyshuvakha, Zaporizhzhia, that killed at least 29 people. I could not independently check Russia's account of these attacks.

### Weapons for Ukraine

The US Air Force has asked industry how to mass-produce the Extended Range Attack Munition (ERAM), a low-cost cruise missile, for Ukraine. The request covers more than 1,000 missiles with a range above 250 nautical miles. Responses are due on 19 November. Deliveries would start within three months of a contract award and finish within 15 months (Defense News).

> **Background — ERAM:** The Trump administration announced a sale of 3,350 ERAMs to Ukraine in August 2025, worth $825 million. This new request is about finding manufacturers able to build them.

### Nordic worries and cyber

Sweden and Finland are weighing how to counter Russian behaviour in the Baltic without escalating, Defense News reports. Russia's Interior Ministry says it dismantled the VasyGrek cybercrime group's infrastructure and detained three suspects.

## Explained

### The EU "national escape clause"
It is a safeguard clause in the EU's fiscal rules. Normally a country must keep its deficit under 3% of GDP and follow an agreed spending path. Under the clause, a government asks Brussels to exclude extra defence spending from that path. The Commission gives an opinion, then finance ministers vote. Think of it as a household allowed to buy a new roof without it counting against the monthly budget. Italy applied on 11 September, the Commission approved on 8 October, and the ministers' vote is expected around 9 November. The figure to remember is about 0.3% of GDP each for defence and energy in 2027 and 2028, within a 1.5% ceiling.

### Real yield
A bond's headline yield is the interest it pays. The real yield subtracts inflation, so it shows what your money actually buys later. Take the 10-year BTP yield of 4.43% and subtract eurozone inflation of 4.0%. A saver earns about 0.4% a year before tax. It is like a bucket filling at 4.4 litres while 4.0 leak out. This matters because higher inflation erodes BTP income, even as the spread narrows. The number to remember is 0.4%.

### Core versus headline inflation
Headline inflation counts all prices. Core strips out food and energy, which swing sharply, to reveal the underlying trend. The ECB watches both: energy can push headline up temporarily, but core and services show whether price rises are spreading. Today, headline is 4.0% but core is 2.5% and services 3.3%. The figure to remember is the ECB's 2% target.

### Cloud rental as an export-control loophole
US rules limit sales of advanced AI chips to China. Chinese firms can rent computing power on foreign cloud servers instead. That is like being barred from buying a car but hiring one by the hour. The Oracle-Tencent deal, reportedly about 100,000 chips for around $7bn, was cancelled under US pressure. That signals rentals are now policed too. The figure to remember is $7bn.

## Glossary

- **Basis point (bp):** one hundredth of a percentage point; 112 bp is 1.12 points.
- **BTP-Bund spread:** the yield gap between Italian and German 10-year bonds, a measure of Italian risk.
- **Eurostat flash estimate:** an early inflation figure that can be revised later.
- **ECB:** the European Central Bank, which sets eurozone interest rates.
- **Ecofin:** the EU finance ministers' council.
- **Strait of Hormuz:** the narrow Gulf waterway through which much of the world's oil and LNG passes.
- **TTF:** the Dutch gas price, Europe's gas benchmark.
- **Confindustria:** Italy's main employers' association.
- **Mimit:** Italy's Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy.
- **PNRR:** Italy's EU-funded recovery plan; the tenth, final instalment is €28.4bn.
- **ERAM:** Extended Range Attack Munition, a low-cost cruise missile being sought for Ukraine.
- **Apec:** the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum, meeting in Shenzhen on 18-19 November.
