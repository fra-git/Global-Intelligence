# Europe & Italy

## Italy

The mood in Rome is nervous. The League's internal fight and high energy costs are the two main domestic pressures, with the 2027 budget as the backdrop. None of it currently threatens the Meloni coalition.

### League revolt: attempt to remove Senate leader Romeo
The League is trying to remove Massimiliano Romeo as its Senate group leader. Avvenire reports that almost all of the party's 29 senators signed against him. Another report puts the signatures at 25 of 29, so the exact count is disputed. I found no confirmed outcome of the group meeting or any resignation.

**Why it matters:** Romeo is secretary of the Lombard League and is seen as close to Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti. He backed Massimo Garavaglia against Matteo Salvini in the party's leadership contest (Il Foglio, 23 October). Opposition parties say the League has put Giorgetti "under trusteeship", which is their reading of events and not an established fact.

**Watch:** whether Romeo resigns or the group votes, and any sign that Giorgetti's budget line is contested.

### Energy bills: businesses and households squeezed

## European Union

The main story is the race to replace Christine Lagarde at the European Central Bank (ECB). Security worries and a possible US-Russia deal are also pressing on Brussels.

### Two-horse race for ECB president
The deadline for nominations passed at 17:00 on 6 November. The Eurogroup, the euro-area finance ministers' forum, received only two names. They are Klaas Knot of the Netherlands and Pablo Hernández de Cos of Spain, both former heads of their national central banks. The ministers will review the names on Monday 9 November, and EU leaders take the final decision. Lagarde leaves on 7 February 2027, according to our earlier edition.

Bloomberg reports that de Cos said policymakers should stay vigilant on inflation risks from high energy prices. This matters because Lagarde has said the ECB may raise rates again in December, with euro-area inflation near 4%.

> **Background — how the ECB president is chosen:** Euro-area governments nominate, the Eurogroup gives an opinion, and the European Council picks by qualified majority. The European Parliament is consulted and the choice is then formalised. The post is an eight-year, non-renewable term.

**For Europe & Italy:** Italy has not put forward a candidate. As a heavily indebted country, it has an interest in who sets rates and how the ECB treats bond-market stress. Markets read a Dutch candidate as more hawkish and a Spanish one as more centrist, but that is analyst opinion and not a fact.

**Watch:** the Eurogroup on 9 November and the 17 December ECB meeting.

## Italy in the EU & PNRR

Brussels is the key audience for Italy's budget and for the final payment of its recovery plan. Italy remains in the EU's excessive deficit procedure, the process for countries that breach the 3%-of-GDP deficit limit.

### Deficit procedure and the Commission's budget opinion
Italy missed an early exit from the procedure. Istat put the 2025 deficit at 3.1% of GDP, just above the limit (Brussels Signal). The Bank of Italy has warned that deficits above 3% in 2027 and 2028 could complicate an exit. The Commission's spring forecast saw 2.9% for 2027, while a Reuters report on Italy's own plan cited 3.4%, so the two figures differ. The Commission normally issues opinions on draft budgets in late November. Last year's was on 25 November, and it was positive.

**Watch:** the Commission's opinion on Italy's 2027 plan, and the Eurogroup on 9 November.

On Friday Trump named Stephen Miller White House chief of staff, starting Monday 9 November. He replaces Susie Wiles, who is moving to Trump's political operation. ### Strong jobs data makes a December Fed hike more likely
Il Sole 24 Ore reports that US employers added 165,000 jobs in October, against a consensus of about 65,000. Unemployment fell to 4.1% from 4.2%. Wages rose 3.1% year on year. August and September were revised up by a combined 84,000. Third-quarter GDP grew at a 3.4% annualised rate, driven by consumer spending and AI-related investment.

**Why it matters:** The Fed's policy rate is 3.75%–4.00%. Because the labour force is shrinking, the economy needs only about 40,000 jobs a month to keep pace, so 165,000 points to overheating. CME FedWatch, which tracks futures pricing, shows about a 77% chance of a December hike, up from about 67% days earlier. That is market pricing, not a Fed decision.

**For Europe & Italy:** Higher US rates can strengthen the dollar and push up global borrowing costs. They add to the pressure on the ECB, which is also weighing a December hike.

**Market impact:** Chip stocks rebounded on Friday after Thursday's AI rout, even as the data reinforced rate-hike bets.

### Washington accuses Beijing of breaching the Busan trade deal

## Fact Check

No fact-checker I could find has yet examined the three claims below. They are checked here against reporting and official statements.

### The claim: "Russia is behind the European bomb-threat wave"
Circulated in social posts and some commentary after the 3-4 November flight and school threats.

**Verdict:** Unproven

**What is actually true:** The FT reports that security agencies are investigating whether the hoaxes started in Russia. Their working hypothesis is pro-Kremlin activists or hackers. Reporting on school threats in Hungary and Slovenia points to a Russian-language account. Al Jazeera notes that no government has publicly blamed any country, and Moscow and Tehran deny hybrid campaigns. Bomb hoaxes are cheap, so many actors could send them.

**Why it matters:** Pro-Ukraine and anti-Russian accounts want the threats pinned on Moscow. Attribution without evidence can be repeated as fact.

*Sources: [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/0845687f-1f74-4bbf-88c9-5c6b031c7f20?syn-25a6b1a6=1), [Al Jazeera](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/4/bomb-threats-disrupt-flights-across-europe-no-explosive-devices-found)*

### The claim: hackers stole 23 TB from Russia's statistics agency
Spread via the hacker groups' own channels and picked up by tech outlets.

**Verdict:** Unproven

**What is actually true:** Cyber Partisans claim more than 10 TB and Silent Crow about 23 TB, so their numbers do not match. Rosstat confirms attacks but denies any database compromise or leak of protected data. Neither side has been independently verified. Bloomberg confirms the disruption: reports are delayed.

**Why it matters:** Hacktivist groups inflate figures for publicity. Moscow also has reasons to minimise damage.

**Why it matters:** Both Moscow and Kyiv have reasons to exaggerate: one to show strength, the other to attract sanctions.

### Investing basics: duration
Duration measures how much a bond's price falls when rates rise. A rough rule: a bond with a duration of seven years loses about 7% if yields rise one percentage point. Long BTPs and long Treasuries therefore swing more than short ones. With central banks hiking, duration is the risk to understand before the yield. Spreading maturities and holding some short-dated paper reduces that risk. It does not remove it, and a bond held to maturity still faces inflation and default risk.

## Explained

### Gas price cap and the TTF benchmark
**What it is:** TTF (Title Transfer Facility) is the Dutch hub price that sets Europe's wholesale gas benchmark. A cap is a ceiling on what the state lets that price feed into bills.

**How it works:** Traders buy and sell gas for future delivery at the hub. Suppliers pass the price to customers, and a cap makes the state pay or fix any difference. In Italy, Confcommercio says a €58.9/MWh cap in the "Bollette bis" decree could cut bills by up to 10%.

**Analogy:** A thermostat limit on a radiator you cannot turn off: the state absorbs the heat above the line.

**Why now:** TTF is about €85/MWh, well above the cap level, so the cap matters only if the state pays the gap.

**Key number:** €58.9/MWh.

### Fed hike odds and CME FedWatch
**What it is:** A tool that turns futures prices into the probability of a rate move.

**How it works:** Traders bet on the average policy rate at a future date. The tool backs out the probabilities. After October's 165,000 jobs it shows about 77% for a December hike, up from about 67%.

**Analogy:** A bookmaker's odds: they show what the crowd is betting, not what the referee will decide.

**Why now:** It drives Treasury yields (10-year 4.86%) and the dollar, and pressures the ECB.

**Key number:** 77%.

### BTP-Bund spread and basis points
**What it is:** The extra yield Italy pays over Germany on ten-year debt, measured in basis points (hundredths of a percentage point).

**How it works:** Investors demand more yield for riskier borrowers. The spread rises when Italian risk, deficits or politics worry markets. German Bunds are the safe benchmark.

**Analogy:** The risk premium a bank adds to your mortgage versus its best customer's.

**Why now:** The spread closed at 107.4 bp and the BTP yield at 4.32% despite League infighting. Italy is in the EU's excessive deficit procedure, with a 2025 deficit of 3.1% of GDP.

**Key number:** 107.4 basis points.

### How the ECB president is chosen
**What it is:** An institutional process for replacing the head of the euro area's central bank.

**How it works:** Governments nominate, the Eurogroup gives an opinion, the European Council decides by qualified majority, and Parliament is consulted. The term is eight years, non-renewable.

**Analogy:** A job shortlist that finance ministers vet before heads of government pick.

**Why now:** Only Knot and Hernández de Cos were nominated by the 6 November deadline. Lagarde leaves on 7 February 2027.

**Key number:** Eight years.

## Glossary

- **ECB:** European Central Bank, which sets interest rates for the euro area.
- **Eurogroup:** Meeting of euro-area finance ministers, which reviews the ECB nominees.
- **BTP:** Italian government bond.
- **Basis point:** One hundredth of a percentage point.
- **TTF:** Dutch gas hub price, Europe's benchmark.
- **Hormuz:** Strait through which a large share of the world's oil passes. It is closed, as reported in earlier editions.
- **Busan deal:** Truce agreed by Trump and Xi in Busan, South Korea, in about October 2025. China suspended rare-earth export controls for a year.
- **Rare earths:** Metals needed for magnets in cars, turbines and weapons. China dominates their processing.
- **NoName057(16):** Pro-Russian hacker group, targeted in Operation Eastwood in July 2025.
- **Golden Dome:** Planned US missile-defence shield.
- **Excessive deficit procedure:** EU process for countries above the 3%-of-GDP deficit limit. Italy is in it.
- **PNRR:** Italy's share of the EU recovery fund. The tenth and final request, €28.4bn, is under Commission review.
