# Evening Update

## What Changed Today

- **Wall Street's worst day in years:** The S&P 500 fell about 4.35% and the Nasdaq Composite about 5.5%, wiping out roughly $3.2tn of market value (ANSA, FT). The trigger was a chip-led rout. Investors fear the Democratic midterm win will bring tighter AI regulation and curbs on data-centre building.
- **Asia opens under pressure:** South Korea's Kospi fell 5.3% on Thursday and quotes now show it below 5,000. Samsung and SK Hynix were already down 7.6% and 9.5% on Thursday (Korea JoongAng Daily). Japan's Nikkei is also sharply lower in early quotes.
- **Europe fell less than the US:** Milan closed down 1%, the worst bourse in Europe. Frankfurt lost 0.4%, London 0.3%, Paris gained 0.3% and Madrid 0.8%. The BTP-Bund spread widened to about 109.7 basis points.
- **Oil rebounds after Wednesday's 9% plunge:** Brent is near $99. Tankers were attacked near Hormuz, and Trump said he will either certify an Iran deal or "destroy the rest of the country", adding "you will know soon".
- **Trump meets Putin's envoy:** He saw Kirill Dmitriev at the White House but not Ukrainian representatives. Melania Trump hosted Russian and Ukrainian officials to mark the Oleshky truce. The Abu Dhabi talks are due on 10-11 November.
- **Lagarde: the December ECB decision is open:** She said the ECB will judge in December whether to hike again and that energy-driven inflation will last for some time.
- **Blue Origin delays launches to 2027:** The delay follows a "devastating explosion" (FT). It leaves SpaceX further ahead.
- **Mexico cuts rates by surprise:** Banxico cut to 6.25%, a four-year low, and signalled an extended hold.

## Europe & Italy

**Italy's markets took the brunt in Europe.** Milan fell 1% as chip and industrial names dropped. STMicroelectronics and Technoprobe lost about 9% each, and Fincantieri, Stellantis and Prysmian about 4%. Leonardo fell 3.4%. Bper lost 2.7% after its third-quarter results, while Tenaris rose 3.6% on its results (ANSA). The 10-year BTP yield closed at 4.33%, with the spread to the German Bund at about 109.7 basis points, up from 105 at the open.

### Lagarde: December decides on another hike
Speaking to students in Milan, ECB President Christine Lagarde said the bank discussed raising rates at its last meeting but chose to wait for data. It will revisit its projections in December. She said energy-driven inflation will probably persist until base effects ease year-on-year comparisons, but called that a probability, not a certainty. ANSA reports she is leaving the ECB earlier than planned so she can speak more freely.

**Why it matters:** Euro-area inflation was 4.0% in October. Energy prices are up about 18% on the year. The ECB is weighing a second tightening in a row against weakening growth.

**For Europe & Italy:** Higher rates raise the cost of servicing Italy's debt, which is why the spread is watched closely.

**Watch:** The ECB's December meeting and updated projections.

*Sources: [ANSA](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/economia/2026/11/05/lagarde-a-dicembre-capiremo-se-dovremo-alzare-ancora-i-tassi_b56dd0c3-738f-4e14-8246-ce1812ffc86d.html)*

### German carmakers hit by four profit warnings
Handelsblatt says VW, BMW and Mercedes issued four profit warnings between them. Third-quarter profits collapsed. The paper blames an electric-vehicle boom in Europe that is squeezing combustion-engine sales, and a collapse of combustion-car demand in China. It sees no improvement ahead.

**Why it matters:** Autos are the core of German industry and a large part of Italy's component supply chain.

*Sources: [Handelsblatt](https://www.handelsblatt.com/unternehmen/industrie/autobauer-die-elektrowelle-ueberrollt-vw-bmw-und-mercedes/100265334.html)*

### Industry lobbies urge ambitious EU budget spending on competitiveness
Confindustria, Germany's BDI and France's Medef jointly asked that negotiations on the next EU budget keep "ambitious" competitiveness spending. They also asked not to raise the fiscal burden on firms.

*Sources: [Il Sole 24 Ore](https://www.ilsole24ore.com/art/confindustria-bdi-medef-rafforzare-spesa-pubblica-europea-imprese-AJqF1t5B)*

## World

### Trump keeps pressure on Iran
Trump said he will either certify an agreement with Iran or "destroy the rest of the country", adding that he would prefer not to (ANSA). He gave no date and the report does not specify the agreement. Hormuz remains disrupted. The Revolutionary Guards say two tankers and a tugboat caught fire, and a mine hit a tanker, according to US media. Qatar-led mediation continues. Brent fell about 9% on Wednesday and rebounded on Thursday. It was heading for its biggest weekly loss since April (Bloomberg).

**Watch:** Any Trump decision, and whether Qatar's phased proposal advances.

### Russia-Ukraine: a White House show of diplomacy
Melania Trump hosted Russian and Ukrainian officials to mark the Oleshky local truce. CNN and US media identify them as the two countries' human-rights ombudsmen. She said nearly 800 people were evacuated. Trump met Dmitriev, Putin's envoy, but not Ukrainian representatives (Bloomberg). Separately, Ukraine's chargé d'affaires hopes for a new Starshield contract with SpaceX by mid-2027 and is weighing alternatives.

**Watch:** The three-way talks in Abu Dhabi on 10-11 November.

### United States: AI politics reprices markets
Democratic Rep. Subramanyam called for a federal data-centre plan to protect communities from higher power costs. Google, Meta and TikTok sued California over a law restricting "psychologically exploitative" features for children. The US also sanctioned four people and 41 companies tied to Cambodia's Prince Group scam network.

### China and Indo-Pacific
The FT says Beijing's block of SoftBank's $5.4bn ABB robotics deal marks the start of a "robot war" over AI-driven machines. China and Taiwan are also contesting the 160th anniversary of Sun Yat-sen's birth (Economist). The Kospi closed down 5.3% on Thursday and is now below 5,000. Software group Atlassian fell about 20% on a weak outlook.

### Middle East
Israel's election on 27 October gave two Arab-majority lists a record 19 seats. Both back Gadi Eisenkot to form a government (Japan Times). In Turkey, former AKP deputy chair Fatma Betul Sayan Kaya was arrested in a fund-manipulation probe (Anadolu).

### Africa
A Nigerian high court sacked the leadership of the opposition ADC party, putting Atiku Abubakar's presidential bid in doubt (BBC). The FT says the ANC is now a minority in South Africa's largest cities.

### Latin America
Brazil approved its first critical minerals list, led by rare earths and copper (Bloomberg). Pope Leo began a trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru weeks after lung surgery (BBC). Argentina's Milei is easing fiscal pressure on small firms.
