## What It Means for You in Italy

- **Mortgages:** An ECB hike in December (17th) is possible but not decided. Variable-rate mortgages would get dearer with a lag. The ECB's deposit rate is reported at 2.50%, so a 25-basis-point rise would add roughly €25 a year per €100,000 borrowed on a variable-rate loan.
- **Inflation and bills:** Lagarde puts Italian inflation above 5%, with energy up more than 20% on the year. She expects pressure to last until base effects fade. Expect no quick relief.
- **Gas and heating:** Italian storage is just above 90%, the highest among the large EU countries. That cushions the winter, but Germany, at 60%, will compete for LNG cargoes.
- **Fuel:** Brent is down 16% on the week, but Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries keep diesel prices high. Pump prices may fall only slowly.
- **Savings and BTPs:** The 10-year BTP yields about 4.3% and the spread is near 110 basis points, calm despite the equity rout. Funds and pension products with heavy US tech exposure are likely to show losses this week.
- **Budget and pensions:** League figures want €850m added to the pension fund so people can retire at 64. Nothing is agreed, and the budget has yet to pass parliament.
- **Travel:** No direct effect reported today. The dollar is stronger, with EUR/USD near 1.12, which makes US trips slightly dearer.

# Europe & Italy

## Italy

Italy's main political story is a revolt inside the League, the junior coalition party led by Matteo Salvini. Markets are also under pressure from a global sell-off in AI-linked shares.

### League turmoil spreads to the budget and the Senate
Most of the League's 29 senators have signed a request to remove Senate group leader Massimiliano Romeo, according to Il Sole 24 Ore. Romeo backed Massimo Garavaglia's bid in the party congress and refuses to resign, saying he will wait for a group assembly. Marco Dreosto is seen as the likeliest replacement. The party's federal council meets in Milan today for the first time since the congress. Routine appointments are expected, and Salvini may delay naming his new leadership team.

The budget is also affected. Alberto Gusmeroli replaced Silvana Comaroli, an ally of Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti, as rapporteur on the budget bill. Party figures have asked Giorgetti to add €850 million to the three-year pension fund so that people can retire at 64.

**Why it matters:** The row pits the party's northern wing against Salvini, and it is now landing on Giorgetti, the League's most market-trusted minister. The coalition is not at risk, but the budget will be harder to manage.

**Watch:** The outcome of the federal council, and whether Romeo's group assembly is called.

*Sources: [Il Sole 24 Ore](https://www.ilsole24ore.com/art/lega-relatore-manovra-capogruppo-senato-tutte-tensioni-partito-AJfFEQ6B)*

Milan opened down 1.68% (Ftse Mib) and traded about 1% lower, with banks weak. Frankfurt and London fell about 2%, and Tokyo's Nikkei closed down 5.96%, below 60,000 for the first time since May. ANSA reports that US stocks lost about $3.2 trillion in value on 5 November. The trigger was fear of tougher rules on AI and data-centre construction.

The BTP-Bund spread (the yield gap between Italian and German 10-year bonds) opened at 110 basis points, against 109 at Thursday's close. The BTP yield was 4.31%. Banks drew attention too. **Market impact:** The spread has barely moved despite the equity sell-off. The euro was down about 0.2% against the dollar.

Speaking to students at Piazza Affari on 5 November, ECB President Christine Lagarde said the ECB will decide in December whether to raise rates again. ## European Union

### The race to succeed Lagarde at the ECB opens
Euro-area governments had until today to put forward candidates for ECB president. Eurogroup chief Kyriakos Pierrakakis plans to review the names at the Eurogroup meeting on 9 November. The ECB said on 29 October that Lagarde will leave on 7 February 2027. Earlier reporting named Pablo Hernández de Cos (Spain's pick, now head of the Bank for International Settlements) and former Dutch central banker Klaas Knot as frontrunners. Bundesbank head Joachim Nagel was also mentioned. Bloomberg asks whether it will be a two-horse race.

**Why it matters:** The Eurogroup only advises. The final decision rests with the European Council, which brings EU leaders together. The choice will shape policy during an inflation spike, and it is also a Franco-German bargaining chip. A separate reported deadline of 27 November covers the seat vacated by Isabel Schnabel.

**Watch:** The list of nominees and the Eurogroup meeting on 9 November.

*Sources: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-06/ecb-deadline-will-show-if-lagarde-succession-is-a-two-horse-race), [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/11/03/brussels-sets-tight-deadline-for-ecb-president-nominations/), [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/03/who-will-replace-lagarde-eurozone-picks-nominees-as-race-for-ecb-top-job-begins)*

### Commission accuses TikTok over "rabbit holes"
The Commission said today that TikTok has failed to stop its recommendation algorithm from pulling users into "rabbit holes" of content about sadness, self-harm and body image. This is a preliminary finding under the Digital Services Act (DSA), the EU's platform law. TikTok has already contested earlier findings on addictive design, calling them "meritless". A final non-compliance decision can bring fines of up to 6% of global turnover.

**Watch:** TikTok's reply, and whether the Commission moves to a final decision.

*Sources: [The Record](https://therecord.media/eu-accuses-tiktok-of-rabbit-hole-DSA-violation)*

## Energy & Critical Minerals

Oil has fallen sharply on the week but remains near $100, and European gas is still about three times its normal level.

### Ukraine's refinery campaign intensifies as oil eases
Bloomberg reports Ukraine hit a sixth Russian refinery in a week, and a major plant halted operations. Brent is $98.46 and WTI $90.83. Brent is down 16.3% on the week, after a 9% drop on 4 November on hopes the Iran war could end sooner. Hormuz remains closed, so the price still carries a large war premium.

**Why it matters:** Strikes on Russian refining cut the diesel and petrol Russia can export. That keeps diesel prices high even if crude falls. Trump met a Russian envoy on Thursday and talked of a coming deal on the Ukraine war (Handelsblatt). The Abu Dhabi talks on 10-11 November are the next test. Peace could ease energy prices further, though no terms are known.

**Market impact:** Falling crude hurts oil producers and helps importers such as India, whose Nifty 50 rose 2.01% on Thursday.

### Gas storage drains in the cold
EU storage fell from 74.56% to 73.75% in three days, to 835.1 TWh, as the weather turned colder (ANSA). Germany is at 60.25% and Italy at 90.08%, the highest of the large countries. Dutch TTF gas is €88.63 per MWh, down 2.26% on the day. Asian LNG hit about $32 per million BTU, close to its highest since 2022, because of reduced shipping through Hormuz.

**For Europe & Italy:** Italy's full storage is a cushion, but Germany's low level raises competition for LNG cargoes. Italy's regulator Arera approved a social bonus for district heating.

### Climate politics
The IEA chief predicts a climate-action comeback before COP31 in Antalya (FT). Germany backs a global electricity target for 2035. Copernicus says 2026 will almost certainly exceed 1.5°C. Copper is $6.27 per pound, down 2.95% on the week.

**Driver:** Gold is $4,079, up 4.6% on the week, as investors seek safety. **Risks:** It pays no income, and a strong dollar or higher real yields can hit it. It also fell 0.83% on Friday.

### Investing basics: drawdowns and concentration

## Scenarios

### The AI sell-off
- **Base case:** Volatility stays high, with partial rebounds, while investors wait for concrete Democratic proposals. This is the most likely path.
- **Upside:** Democratic leaders play down data-centre curbs and spending plans hold. The rebound extends, which is plausible.
- **Downside:** Specific bills or state-level limits emerge, or more chipmakers disappoint. Selling spreads to credit and Europe, which is less likely but possible.
- **Signposts:** Statements from Democratic leaders, US CPI on 10 November, and the VIX (now 26.88).

### ECB rates and the succession
- **Base case:** The ECB holds the line until December and then raises by 25 basis points if the inflation projections stay high. This is likely.
- **Upside:** Energy prices fall with Iran diplomacy, the ECB pauses and Italian borrowing costs ease. This is possible.
- **Downside:** Inflation broadens and a hike is followed by more, while the succession becomes a Franco-German standoff. This is less likely.
- **Signposts:** The list of nominees, oil prices, and the December projections.

- **Base case:** Talks continue with no signed deal, and Hormuz stays closed in the near term. This is likely.

- **Downside:** Tehran's flexibility proves tactical, missile rebuilding continues, and Russia only pretends to negotiate, as Lithuania's president warns. This is plausible.

## Explained

### Basis points, the BTP-Bund spread and the ECB
- **What it is:** The spread is the extra yield Italy pays over Germany on 10-year bonds. A basis point is 0.01 percentage point.
- **How it works:** Investors treat Germany as the safe benchmark. They demand extra yield to hold Italian debt. If worry about Italy rises, the spread widens and the Treasury's borrowing gets dearer.
- **Analogy:** It is the risk premium on a loan to a less-trusted neighbour.
- **Why now:** Despite the global sell-off, the spread opened at 110, against 109 on Thursday, with the BTP at 4.31%.
- **Key number:** 110 basis points.

### Digital Services Act enforcement
- **What it is:** The EU's platform law lets the Commission police very large platforms.
- **How it works:** The Commission investigates and issues preliminary findings. The company replies. A final non-compliance decision can follow, with fines up to 6% of global turnover.
- **Analogy:** A regulator's formal charge sheet before the verdict.
- **Why now:** TikTok faces findings on its algorithm after the Commission analysed about 20,000 videos.
- **Key number:** 6%.

### Data-centre regulation risk
- **What it is:** Data centres are the power-hungry buildings that run AI.
- **How it works:** AI valuations assume rapid construction. Limits on permits, power or local approvals could slow it, and with it chip and equipment demand.
- **Analogy:** Rules on building new motorways would hit the carmakers' growth plans.
- **Why now:** Fear of such limits triggered the $3tn sell-off, though no law has passed.
- **Key number:** 6.36% (the Nasdaq 100's fall on Thursday).

### Impeachment
- **What it is:** The House can charge an official by simple majority. Removal needs two-thirds of the Senate.
- **How it works:** Articles are voted in the House, then the Senate holds a trial.
- **Analogy:** The House indicts, the Senate judges.
- **Why now:** Massie wants to revive articles against Hegseth.
- **Key number:** Two-thirds.

## Glossary

- **Basis point:** one hundredth of a percentage point.
- **BTP:** Italian government bond.
- **Spread:** the yield gap between Italian and German 10-year bonds.
- **Eurogroup:** euro-area finance ministers, who advise on ECB appointments.
- **Ecofin:** the Council of all EU finance ministers.
- **ECB deposit rate:** the rate banks earn on cash parked at the ECB, the main policy rate.
- **Copasir:** the Italian parliamentary committee overseeing intelligence.
- **DSA:** the EU's Digital Services Act.
- **Hormuz:** the strait through which much Gulf oil and LNG passes.
- **PNRR:** Italy's EU-funded recovery plan, with a €28.4bn final instalment pending.
- **VIX:** Wall Street's volatility index.
- **Brent:** the international oil benchmark.
