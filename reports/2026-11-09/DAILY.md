# Europe & Italy

## Italy

Italian assets rallied on Monday as oil fell and banks surged. The government is also dealing with a wave of pro-Russian cyber activity and preparing a reform of the provinces.

### Milan jumps 2% on banks as oil falls
Milan's FTSE MIB rose about 2% by midday to above 51,000 points. Banco BPM jumped on bank consolidation talk ("risiko bancario"), and UniCredit rose after its results. The BTP-Bund spread, the yield gap between Italian and German 10-year bonds, was 106-108 basis points. The Italian 10-year yield was 4.31% at the open. Brent crude fell about 3.5% to below $95 after unconfirmed reports that Iran had softened its demands over the Strait of Hormuz.

**Why it matters:** The spread is a gauge of the extra return investors demand to hold Italian rather than German debt. It is stable near recent levels, and lower energy prices help Italy, a large gas and oil importer.

**Market impact:** Banks led the rise. Dutch TTF gas, the European benchmark, opened up 1.2% at €86.63/MWh. Bloomberg reports European storage could be at its lowest in more than a decade by spring.

**Watch:** Banking deal announcements, and whether the Hormuz reports are confirmed.

## European Union

### Finance ministers open the race to succeed Lagarde at the ECB
Euro-zone finance ministers began a first assessment of the European Central Bank presidency. Spain has nominated Pablo Hernández de Cos, head of the Bank for International Settlements. The Netherlands has nominated Klaas Knot, a former Dutch central bank governor. Handelsblatt says the timetable is tight and a decision should come soon, followed by other top appointments. Lagarde is reported to be leaving in February 2027. EIB president Nadia Calviño told Bloomberg that the successor must follow Lagarde's approach.

**Why it matters:** The ECB president sets the tone for interest rates for 20 countries. Analysts quoted by Reuters-linked outlets see the two as close on policy, Knot slightly more hawkish (favouring tighter policy) and de Cos slightly more dovish. The choice is formally made by EU leaders, so it is also a political bargain over top jobs.

**For Europe & Italy:** Italy, whose borrowing costs depend on ECB policy, has no candidate and will be a swing vote in the bargaining.

**Watch:** The next Eurogroup and Ecofin statements, and any move by Germany, which has been floated through Bundesbank chief Joachim Nagel.

## Italy in the EU & PNRR

Italy is still in the EU's excessive deficit procedure. In June the Commission said Rome had taken effective measures but kept the procedure open. Italian press put the 2025 deficit at 3.1% of GDP, per Eurostat. The 2027 budget plan is reported to target a deficit of 2.8%. The Commission's formal opinion on Italy's draft budget has not appeared in my searches. Under past practice it comes in the late-November autumn package. Italian press also reported a first Commission green light to use the fiscal "escape clause" for extra defence and energy spending (ItaliaOggi cites €28.8bn). I could not verify that figure independently.

On Monday Italy's position in Brussels mattered mainly for the ECB race. Spain and the Netherlands have candidates, and Italy has none.

### PNRR tracker
The PNRR is Italy's share of the EU recovery fund, worth €194.4bn in total. Italy submitted its tenth and final payment request on 29 September. It is worth €28.4bn and covers 156 targets that, according to Minister Tommaso Foti, have been reached. The Commission is now assessing the request. Italy had received its ninth instalment on 3 June, bringing the total received to about €166bn (Chamber of Deputies figures). I found no news today of the Commission's assessment.

| 9th instalment | Paid in June | Nothing outstanding |
| EU programme deadline | Payments must end in 2026 | Closing audits and any reprogramming |

# Great Powers

## United States

Democrats won control of Congress in the 3 November midterms. Scripps News reported at least 228 House seats, against 218 needed for a majority. Hakeem Jeffries is positioned to become Speaker in January. Trump said on social media that he was not on the ballot. Voters cited high prices, the conflict involving Iran and corruption allegations.

### Democratic Congress puts Trump's tariffs under pressure
The new majority takes office in January, so Trump's tariffs and other policies will face scrutiny. The FT argues the Democrats may be content to reverse the duties on Canada, a "partial return to tariff sanity". Bloomberg Economics says the vote signalled populist priorities on cost of living. Tariffs are a likely flashpoint because they raise consumer prices.

**Why it matters:** Congress holds the budget and oversight powers. A hostile House can open investigations and block spending. Tariffs, however, rest largely on executive authority, so Trump can still act without Congress.

**For Europe & Italy:** Europe is under a US tariff regime and wants it stable. A Congress that questions Trump's trade moves could reduce the risk of new escalation. It cannot by itself remove existing duties on EU goods. The Italian export sectors most exposed are machinery, food and luxury goods.

**Watch:** The final seat counts, and whether Democrats move to curb tariffs through votes or hearings once sworn in.

*Sources: [Scripps News](https://www.scrippsnews.com/politics/america-votes/washington-faces-new-reality-after-democratic-wave-victory), [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/a86de4ff-f3de-4156-8ffa-bde10783d8b1?syn-25a6b1a6=1), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-11-09/trump-tariffs-and-american-populism)*

## China

China's economy is sending mixed signals. Factory-gate inflation is climbing while demand stays weak, and Beijing is trying to keep the trade truce with Washington on track before a Xi–Trump meeting.

### Beijing extends its tariff pause with the US for two months
China will suspend retaliatory measures, including an additional 24% tariff on US goods, for another two months. This formalises a leaders' decision to prolong the truce until January. Bloomberg says a 10% rate stays in place. Soybean traders are watching for Chinese farm purchases, which Washington is pressing for.

**Why it matters:** The truce dates from the October 2025 Busan meeting. A lapse would have meant new tariffs and possible curbs on rare earths, which Europe's carmakers and defence firms also depend on. Other reports say the new deadline is 10 January 2027. Tariffs, rare earths and tech curbs are still unresolved.

**Market impact:** Soybean futures erased earlier gains as the pause was offset by expectations of a large US crop.

**Watch:** The Xi–Trump meeting, due a little over a week away according to Bloomberg.

*Sources: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/china-extends-pause-on-24-us-tariffs-to-january-keeps-10-rate), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/soybeans-rise-as-us-presses-china-to-boost-farm-goods-purchases)*

### Producer prices hit a four-year high but demand stays weak
Producer inflation accelerated to the fastest since June 2022 (Bloomberg's headline gives 5.7%), driven by costlier energy. Consumer-price growth slowed. The FT calls it weak domestic demand amid an energy crisis. Bloomberg Economics labels this "bad inflation": costs rise but demand does not support higher prices, so deflation risks remain.

**Why it matters:** Higher input costs squeeze margins without lifting spending. That limits Beijing's room for stimulus.

*Sources: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/chinese-producer-inflation-soars-to-5-7-for-post-covid-era-high), [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/085c4610-143a-47ad-b546-0587695294b5?syn-25a6b1a6=1)*

### Beijing calls the Nvidia spy claims "smears"
China dismissed allegations that it spied on Nvidia's CEO as baseless. A Chinese newspaper warned on ties, per Bloomberg. Handelsblatt reports that investors are pulling money from Chinese AI stocks despite state investment.

**Watch:** Whether the episode hardens US chip-export policy before the summit.

*Sources: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/china-newspaper-dismisses-jensen-huang-spy-claim-warns-on-ties), [Handelsblatt](https://www.handelsblatt.com/finanzen/maerkte/aktienmarkt-chinas-ki-boom-bekommt-risse/100267523.html)*

## Russia & Ukraine

Diplomacy and fighting are running in parallel. Trilateral talks among the US, Ukraine and Russia are due in Abu Dhabi, with reports pointing to 10–11 November. Russia is hitting Ukrainian energy and Ukraine is hitting Russian refineries.

### Europeans join the Abu Dhabi talks
Politico reports that the EU is sending two senior officials, from the European Commission and the Council, to the talks. The FT says the Europeans want to thwart Russian security demands. Politico's Brussels Playbook says the stakes are high, with Europe's future in the balance.

**Why it matters:** Until now Europe has largely watched talks between Washington, Moscow and Kyiv from outside. Any deal on security guarantees or troop deployments would fall largely on Europeans to implement. Territory has been the central dispute in earlier rounds: Moscow has demanded that Ukraine leave Donbas.

## Explained

### The BTP–Bund spread
- **What it is:** The gap between the yields on Italian and German 10-year bonds. Germany is the euro area's safest borrower, so the gap measures the extra return investors demand for Italian risk.
- **How it works:** A yield rises when a bond's price falls. If investors worry about Italy's debt or politics, they sell BTPs, the yield rises, and the spread widens. Higher spreads raise the state's borrowing cost, which feeds into banks' funding costs.
- **Analogy:** The extra interest a bank charges a riskier borrower than a safer one.
- **Today:** The 10-year BTP yields about 4.31% and the Bund 3.24%, giving a spread of about 106 points.
- **Key number:** 106 basis points, where 100 basis points equal one percentage point.

### Takeover bids in Italian banking
- **What it is:** A takeover bid (OPAS, a mixed public offer) is a public offer to shareholders to buy or swap their shares. A friendly merger proposal is made to the board.
- **How it works:** The bidder files with regulators. After approval, an acceptance period opens. Shareholders tender their shares, and the bid succeeds only if enough accept.
- **Analogy:** An auction in which everyone is told the price in advance.
- **Today:** Intesa's offer for MPS was cleared, with acceptance opening 24 November. Banco BPM rose about 10% on Crédit Agricole's proposal.
- **Key number:** 24 November.

### Export controls on AI chips
- **What it is:** US rules that limit which advanced chips can be sold to China.
- **How it works:** Washington licenses sales. Intermediaries who rent chip capacity abroad can create loopholes, which is why brokers such as Megaspeed attract scrutiny.
- **Analogy:** A lock on a door that someone may be renting out the key to.
- **Today:** The Nvidia affair has revived questions about who accessed the chips.
- **Key number:** About $200bn of Nvidia market value lost.

### Fiscal truce and tariff suspension
- **What it is:** A temporary pause in tariffs agreed between leaders.
- **How it works:** Each side suspends retaliation for a fixed period. Deadlines force repeated renewals.
- **Analogy:** A ceasefire that must be renewed.
- **Today:** China keeps a 24% extra tariff suspended to January and maintains a 10% rate.
- **Key number:** 10 January 2027.
