# Evening Update

## What Changed Today

- **Oil falls to a 10-week low:** Brent closed at $92.21 a barrel, down 6.2%, and WTI at $86.35. The FT tied the fall to Trump calling strikes on Iran "highly unlikely". ANSA cited possible progress in the Middle East. No Iran deal is confirmed.

- **Saudi pipeline hit:** The FT reports an attack on Saudi Arabia's East-West crude pipeline, a key export route that avoids the Strait of Hormuz.
- **US inflation test:** US consumer price data are due Tuesday, with Treasury yields in focus. The US 10-year yield was 4.86%.

## Europe & Italy

### Milan leads Europe as banks rally, spreads narrow
Piazza Affari closed up about 2.6%, the best performance in Europe, on the credit sector (ANSA). Banco BPM rose 10% after Crédit Agricole's offer. UniCredit gained about 6% on its results. The BTP-Bund spread fell to 103 basis points, with the 10-year BTP yielding 4.29%. The euro was strong and Eni weakened with oil. Dutch TTF gas fell 3.9% to €82.2/MWh.

**Why it matters:** Crédit Agricole, already the largest shareholder in Banco BPM, is pushing to combine it with its Italian arm. The sector is consolidating quickly.

**Watch:** Terms of the offer and the Italian government's stance.

### France blames Iran's Revolutionary Guards for fridge cyberattacks
In the first attribution since the October attacks, French authorities named Shahid Kaveh, an IRGC cyber unit previously linked to attacks on US water-utility controllers. Foreign Minister Barrot called the attack unprecedented, and the ambassador was summoned. The attacks spoiled food and, in some cases, caused fires.

**Why it matters:** This is a state-level attribution for an attack on civilian infrastructure. Il Sole 24 Ore notes that the pro-Russian groups Z-Pentest and NoName057(16) had claimed the attacks on European fridges. The US Justice Department says Z-Pentest was founded and directed by Russian military intelligence (GRU). The claims are not reconciled, and attribution remains contested.

**For Europe & Italy:** Italy's own cyber debate is heating up. The opposition says the government is cutting €15m a year from the national cybersecurity strategy fund. The majority says the money goes to defence.

**Watch:** Whether Paris announces sanctions. Interior Minister Piantedosi's security committee meets Tuesday.

*Sources: [The Record](https://therecord.media/france-blames-iran-for-fridge-cyberattacks), [Handelsblatt](https://www.handelsblatt.com/politik/international/botschafter-einbestellt-frankreich-sieht-iran-hinter-angriff-auf-kuehlanlagen/100268528.html), [Il Sole 24 Ore](https://www.ilsole24ore.com/art/z-pentest-gruppo-hacker-russo-diretto-gru-dietro-attacchi-sistemi-industriali-europei-e-usa-AJDor09B), [ANSA](https://www.ansa.it/sito/notizie/politica/2026/11/09/minaccia-hacker-e-il-governo-taglia-il-cyber-lopposizione-attacca_31f21c46-07ed-48a7-af7d-521be9175675.html)*

### Meloni advances a gas-plant reimbursement to cut power bills
Italy moved ahead with a plan to reimburse gas-fired power plants for part of their fuel costs, Bloomberg reports. Premier Giorgia Meloni wants to limit the effect of high energy prices before next year's general election.

**Why it matters:** In Italy gas sets the electricity price much of the time, so lowering the plants' cost aims to lower wholesale power prices. The cost is expected to appear elsewhere, for example in charges on bills, and the final details are not confirmed.

*Sources: [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-09/meloni-backs-gas-price-cap-to-curb-bills-ahead-of-italy-election)*

## World

### Russia and Ukraine: talks resume while Russia targets bridges
A new round of US-brokered talks is due to start Tuesday. Russian and Ukrainian envoys are expected to discuss ceasefire monitoring and territory. It is the first such round since February, when talks were paused as Washington turned to the Iran war (Japan Times). India held high-level calls with both Moscow and Kyiv as it seeks a bigger role (Bloomberg).

On the ground, Zelensky says Russian strikes on the Dnipro bridges aim to cut Ukraine in two, severing power and transport links with the east (FT). Kallas said Russia is "pretending" to negotiate while "bombing civilians". The Economist argues Trump is in a hurry for peace and Putin is not, so the talks may help Russia prolong the war.

**Why it matters:** The bridges are the main routes for moving troops and supplies across the Dnipro. Without them the east becomes hard to supply.

**Watch:** Whether a ceasefire-monitoring format is agreed.
