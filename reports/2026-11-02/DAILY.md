# Europe & Italy

## Italy

Parliament's budget session opens this week. Italian markets opened the month calmer: Milan's FTSE MIB was up about 0.5% in the afternoon, and the BTP-Bund spread (the extra yield Italy pays over Germany) fell to about 116 basis points, from just under 118 at the open (ANSA).

### Budget bill reaches the Chamber as the fiscal fight with Brussels looms
The government approved the 2027 budget bill on 28 October. It is expected in the Chamber of Deputies on Tuesday, with three rapporteurs and €120 million set aside for amendments. Hearings start in about a week, and final approval is due by 31 December. Reported sizes differ: Meloni spoke of a €26 billion package, while another account says €23.4 billion, of which €14.4 billion rests on extra deficit allowed for energy and defence. The bill includes a 33% income-tax bracket up to €55,000. Parliament has also approved a deficit-deviation resolution worth €29 billion over 2027-28, by 219 votes to 110. Economy Minister Giorgetti says he will negotiate that room with the European Commission.

**Why it matters:** This is the last budget before the 2027 general election. Much of it depends on the Commission accepting the extra deficit.

**Market impact:** The spread is the main gauge of investor confidence in Italy's debt. It is lower today, but the budget's deficit path will test it.

**Watch:** The Chamber's budget committee work and the hearings from about 9 November.

### Budget measures on public-sector pay and the cost of living
Il Sole 24 Ore reports that the bill phases out the nine-month wait for public employees' severance pay (TFS). The wait shrinks by three months a year and disappears in 2030. A separate rule in the bill caps the automatic pay rises of magistrates by reintroducing a weighted-average criterion. A Confesercenti-Ipsos poll finds that more than 9 in 10 Italians are cutting spending (53% on restaurants, 41% on holidays). Confesercenti estimates €1.4 billion of consumption is at risk this quarter.

## European Union

The European Central Bank (ECB) held its deposit rate at 2.50% on 29 October, after raising rates by 25 basis points in September. Reports that day said Christine Lagarde will leave early, on 7 February 2027, rather than at the end of her term in October 2027. Today Governing Council member Peter Kazimir said markets may be underestimating the tightening needed, since the energy shock shows no sign of fading and inflation is picking up again (Bloomberg). **Why it matters:** The ECB is raising rates into an energy shock, and its leadership will change in the middle of it.

**For Europe & Italy:** Higher rates raise the cost of servicing Italy's debt and of mortgages. Most Italian mortgages are variable-rate.

**Market impact:** Money markets already price a December hike as likely, per reports citing LSEG data.

**Watch:** The 4 November inflation flash and the December ECB meeting.

Zelensky says US-Ukraine-Russia negotiators are expected to meet in Abu Dhabi on 6–10 November. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said resuming Russian gas exports to Europe via Ukraine is currently not feasible. He expects Trump to keep pursuing a settlement after the midterms. The Wall Street Journal reported the US wants gas transit restored before winter as part of a deal to halt energy strikes. Zelensky said he is ready to meet Putin in Turkey, while Putin offers Moscow. A six-day local truce in occupied Oleshky expired on 1 November. **For Europe & Italy:** Gas via Ukraine ended on 1 January 2025. Any restart would be politically explosive in the EU, which is phasing out Russian gas. FT describes three camps in the West on how to deal with Russia.

**Watch:** Whether the Abu Dhabi round happens, and Black Sea ceasefire talks, which Rubio says are gaining interest.

### Investing basics: duration
Duration measures how sensitive a bond's price is to interest rates. A bond with a duration of 7 years falls roughly 7% if yields rise by one percentage point, and rises about 7% if they fall. When central banks are hiking, long-duration bonds are the most vulnerable. Short-duration ones lose less, but they also offer less protection if rates fall.

## Explained

### Deficit deviation (the €29bn margin)
EU fiscal rules set a path for how fast a country's spending can grow. Italy asks to leave that path for a limited time, for energy and defence. Parliament voted 219 to 110 to ask for €29bn of extra room over 2027-28. The Commission and the Council must then accept it. Think of a speed limit with a temporary exemption that needs the traffic police's approval. The budget's tax cuts partly depend on it. Remember €29bn.

### The BTP-Bund spread
It is the gap between the yield on Italy's 10-year bond and Germany's. Investors demand more yield when they see more risk. Both yields move with the ECB, so the gap isolates Italy-specific worry. It is like the extra interest a bank charges a riskier borrower. At about 116 basis points, it signals calm despite the budget. Remember 116 (one basis point is 0.01 percentage points).

### Why oil shocks push central banks to hike
Rate hikes cool demand, but they cannot make more oil. Central banks hike when higher energy costs spread into wages and prices, risking lasting inflation. It is like putting a lid on a pot that a stove is overheating, which also slows the cooking. The ECB held at 2.50% on 29 October, after a September hike, and signals more may come. Brent near $109 is the key price.

### How crypto laundering works
Hackers who steal crypto move it through many wallets, swap services and decentralised-finance platforms to break the trail. The FBI published wallet addresses so firms can block them. It is like a thief changing banknotes into small coins across many shops. Blockchain analysts estimate about $3bn of the $6.3bn is frozen. Remember 90%, Binance's pledged cover.
